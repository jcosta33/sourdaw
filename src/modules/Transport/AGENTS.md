# Transport module — Agent Guidelines

Playback lifecycle and control (play, stop, seek, record, overdub), playhead positioning & high-frequency scheduler, tempo maps and ramps, time signature maps, metronome, loop/punch regions, master gain headroom, and PPQ/sample position projections; does not own the WebAudio graph (AudioEngine) or arrangement track/clip items (Arrangement).

## Public Contract Surface

- `useCases`:
    - **Playback & Transport Controls**: `setPlayback`, `stopPlayback`, `togglePlayback`, `seekPlayhead`, `panicAllNotes`, `toggleRecording`, `toggleOverdub`, `setLoopRegion`, `toggleLoop`, `disableLooping`, `toggleMetronome`, `setMetronomeVolume`, `setCountInBars`, `toggleCountIn`, `setPreRollBars`, `togglePreRoll`, `setPunchIn`, `setPunchOut`, `togglePunchEnabled`, `createPunchRegionPatch`.
    - **Tempo & Time Signature Mapping**: `setTempo`, `setTimeSignature`, `addTempoChange`, `removeTempoChange`, `updateTempoChange`, `replaceTempoMap`, `resolveTempoAtBeat`, `shiftTimelineMapsAfterBeat`, `prepareTimelineMapStateRestore`, `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation`, `detectProjectTempo`, `adjustTempoPoint`, `addTimeSignatureChange`, `removeTimeSignatureChange`, `replaceTimeSignatureMap`, `getTimeSignatureAtBeat`, `getBarStartBeat`.
    - **Projections & Master Level**: `createMusicalPositionProjector`, `createSamplePositionProjector`, `projectPpqEndpoints`, `secondsBetweenBeats`, `setMasterGain`, `replaceMasterGain`, `ensureTrackStrips`, `getSchedulerTimingDiagnostics`, `reconcileVcaGroupRuntimeGain`, `reconcileVcaRuntimeGain`, `setStopPlaybackCallback`, `restoreTransportSnapshot`, `restoreTimelineMapSnapshot`, `getTransportHandlers`, `getTransportState`, `getTempoMapState`, `resolveTempoFieldState`, `updateTransportState`, `defaultTransportState`.
- `stores`: `transportStore` (`TransportState`, `MIN_TEMPO`, `MAX_TEMPO`), `tempoMapStore` (`TempoMapStoreState`), `timeSignatureMapStore` (`TimeSignatureMapStoreState`), `playheadPositionRef`, and the map readers `readTempoAtBeat`, `readSecondsAtBeat`, `readBeatAtSamples`, `readBarStartBeat` for handlers that cannot reach the use-case barrel.
- Handlers: `getTransportHandlers`.

## Key Subsystems

- **Playhead Scheduler**: Precise audio-clock scheduling loop backed by `playheadPositionRef` for zero-allocation, high-frequency position polling by UI canvas renderers.
- **Tempo Map Engine**: Resolves dynamic tempo changes, linear ramps, and metric beat conversions across project timelines (`useCases/tempoMap/*`).
- **Master Gain & VCA Reconciliation**: Controls master fader gain with headroom constraints, undo integration, and VCA group gain summing.

## Invariants & Traps

- Two tempo changes on one beat mean "arrive at the first, govern from the last", and list order is what says so. `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation` writes the pair when Delete Time cuts a linear ramp: the tempo before the cut never changes, so the ramp keeps its slope up to the cut and arrives at an instant change holding the value it reached, and the tempo steps there to the one carried from the span end. Every reader of the list therefore reads a segment that ends on a shared beat at the first change (the ramp's target) and one that starts there at the last (the governing change): `getTempoAtBeat`, `secondsBetweenBeats`, the Yeast copy of that integrator in `MidiEvent.ts`, `addTempoChange` (which rewrites the last), and `projectEngineTransportMaps` (which opens one engine segment per beat, because the engine refuses segments that do not start on strictly increasing frames). A project saved with a step reads wrongly in a build that predates this rule.
- Delete Time classifies each tempo and meter change against the user's exact bounds `S` and `E`, and `BEAT_EPSILON` applies only to that classification: a change before `S − ε` is kept, one from `S − ε` up to `E − ε` is removed, and one from `E − ε` on shifts by exactly `E − S`, the span the arrangement's clips, markers and sections move by, landing exactly on `S` when it lands within ε of it. The bounds never move to meet a nearby change: a span snapped to a change would shift the maps by a different amount from the content and put every later change and downbeat off its material. After classification every change on the cut sits exactly on `S` and every kept change more than ε before it, so each later "is there a change on the cut" decision compares exactly, and a ramp whose target is removed with the cut is read along its own line up to `S`, never past its target: a target within ε before `S` is where the ramp has already arrived, so it reaches `S` at that target's tempo (its slope before the cut changes by at most `(S − T) / (S − G)` of its rise) rather than overshooting by an extrapolation that a ramp narrower than ε turns into hundreds of BPM.
- Insert Time at `A` for `d` beats never changes the tempo or meter before `A`, moves the material from `A` on by exactly `d` with its tempo, and fills `[A, A + d)` with the tempo arriving at `A` (the value a ramp under way there has reached). Unlike Delete Time it classifies each tempo and meter change exactly, as the arrangement classifies clips and markers: one before `A` is kept and one from `A` on shifts by exactly `d`, with no tolerance. A tolerant classification would move a change a whole inserted span away from the clip or marker sitting on its own beat (a change at `A − 5e-7` would land on `A + d` while its clip stays). When the change kept last before `A` is linear and ramps toward a change at or past `A`, shifting that target alone would re-aim the ramp and slow everything before `A`, so `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation` writes an instant arrival at `A` holding the value the ramp reached there (placed before any change on that beat, so the ramp arrives at it). A target the shift puts past `A + d` gets a linear change at `A + d` holding the same value, which resumes the ramp toward the shifted target on its original slope; a target the shift puts on `A + d` (one on `A`, or a rounding step past it) arrives there itself and governs from there, since a continuation on its beat would govern after it. A steady change in force at `A` (instant, flat, or the last change) needs neither, and the insert only shifts.
- High-frequency playhead updates during live playback MUST read from `playheadPositionRef` — NEVER push per-frame playhead positions into `transportStore` or React component state.
- Tempo values must stay within the range their own validator enforces: the transport's base tempo within `MIN_TEMPO` (20 BPM) and `MAX_TEMPO` (300 BPM) owned by `stores/transportStore`, and a tempo-map change within `MIN_TEMPO_MAP_TEMPO` (20 BPM) and `MAX_TEMPO_MAP_TEMPO` (999 BPM) owned by `models/TempoMap`. The two ranges deliberately differ.
- `stopPlayback` is one transport publication: it clears `isRecording` (through `stopActiveRecording`) and `isPlaying` inside one store batch, so a subscriber never sees a rolling, not-recording transport that the user never produced — the state in which Auto input monitoring would close and reopen its edge. A punch-out (`stopActiveRecording` alone) is a real publication of that state.
- All timeline edits that insert or delete time ranges must invoke `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation` (insert holds the tempo arriving at the insert point across the inserted span; delete carries the tempo and meter in force at the span end to its start) to keep tempo and time signature markers synchronized with track content. `shiftTimelineMapsAfterBeat` only shifts the changes past a beat and keeps no ramp, so it is not an Insert Time.
- Stored clip controllers (Grand Boule pedals, Levain controllers) are placed by `projectClipControllerEvents` — the one projection live scheduling and the offline render share — over the same half-open `[fromBeat, toBeat)` window as notes, at the sample frame a note on that beat gets. At one frame the instrument receives releases, then controllers, then note-ons, then note expression (`SAME_FRAME_EVENT_ORDER`), in live playback (`sameFramePostQueue` posts a track's window in that order, so it cannot depend on which window a note started in or which clip came first) and in the offline render (`comparePendingWorkletEvents`) alike, and no note is posted whose release would share its own start frame. The reason: a pedal must catch a note struck at its frame, and sostenuto, una corda and Levain dynamics must apply to the note struck there, but a pedal must not catch a note released at its frame; the engine queues keep posting order within a frame (live MIDI input stamps several performer events on one frame), so the order is made before the post, never inside the queue. Playback that stops or seeks ends its hold on the instruments through `releaseStoredControllers` (reached through `stopPlayheadScheduler`): it first drops the stored moves still queued for a frame that will not come, then lifts every pedal stored playback moved, frameless, and never writes a controller that is not a pedal (CC1, CC7 and CC11 keep the value they last applied). The drop is needed because `allNotesOff` keeps queued Grand Boule pedals and every queued Levain controller (a controller is state, not a note), so a lift or a CC11 move posted for the up-to-0.1 s look-ahead past the old position would otherwise apply after a jump's restore or after the stop. Every move stored playback posts carries a `stored` mark through `grandBouleControls`/`levainControls`, and `discardStoredPedals`/`discardStoredCc` drop only marked moves (queue compaction in place, nothing allocated on the audio thread; a stored frameless move also leaves a performer's queued moves alone), so live MIDI input, whose order within a frame must survive, is never discarded or reordered. Only devices the scheduler itself posted to are in the record, so a pedal the user holds live is never touched. The lift covers every pedal moved, not only those whose last posted move was engaged, because a dropped queued lift leaves the pedal where the last applied move put it, which the post-time record cannot tell. Sending the value in force when playback starts mid-clip (or after a user seek) is the transport's chase, not this projection's. A playback-internal relocation is not a start: a loop wrap (scheduled seam and late wrap) and a follow-action jump open their window with `scheduleMidiNotes(..., opensAtRelocation)`, which sends, at the destination frame, the value in force there for every stored controller of the track (`projectClipControllerRestore`, a track-level chase as a DAW does it: per controller the last move up to the destination across every pass of every clip of the track that plays, a clip that does not span the destination included and projected only up to the destination, which is the value continuous playback holds there, so a pass head that carries nothing (no row of the controller precedes its visible span) leaves the value the previous pass ended on and is never reset, and a clip that follows one without a row for a controller leaves it set; the clips are those the window scheduler would play (`resolveStoredControllerClips`: comping, mute and note-record rules included, in the window's clip order). "Last" is the order the window posts in: by sample frame, then clip sequence, then the clip's own row order, never by float beat, because two rows a rounding step apart in beat share a frame and the instrument ends on the later clip's. A controller is one value per instrument, the post path drops the channel, so it is keyed by number alone: the last row on any channel carries, and a controller whose last move is one the window posts on the destination's sample frame is left to the window, so it is sent once. Every event comes from `projectClipControllerEvents`, the projection the window emits through, and "at the destination" is a sample-frame comparison: the restore never computes a pass, a content beat or a tolerance itself, because any second computation of a beat disagrees with the window by a rounding step when the clip start or content offset is not a dyadic fraction) and returns to rest only what stored playback moved that no row has a value for there: a pedal is lifted, and a Levain CC1, CC2, CC7 or CC11 goes back to the engine's own default (`LEVAIN_CONTROLLER_DEFAULTS`, a mirror of `ExpressionState::new` pinned to the Rust source), because a controller that stays where a pass left it is as stuck as a pedal. Stored playback records the non-pedal controllers it moves as it records pedals. A destination where no clip of the track plays is no different (a gap is not a reset, so the pedal a clip before it left down stays down): the restore runs for the device the window routes stored controllers to, whether or not stored playback has posted to it yet, and the window and the restore share that one decision (`resolveStoredControllerDevice`: the honouring worklet synth, and neither a Yeast, drum-kit nor Toaster route). `releaseUnrestoredStoredControllers` lifts, unconditionally, only what no track restored: a muted or frozen track, or a posted device the scheduler no longer routes stored controllers to. The reason: a relocation stops notes but deliberately keeps pedal state, so without it a sustain pressed late in one pass stays down through every later pass. One rule serves every relocation: a pedal with a row in force is given that value and never lifted first (a Grand Boule releases the voices a lifted sustain was holding, and a press on the same frame does not bring them back). A loop-region or tempo-map edit during playback cuts the look-ahead and re-emits it from where playback stands (or from the loop start), so every arm of that teardown marks the re-emitting window a relocation (`reemitAfterEdit`). The late wrap (both arms), the jump and the edit run `discardStaleStoredMoves` before that window, which drops the stored moves queued for the old position and nothing else, so none applies over the restore; the scheduled seam never does, because its dying window's own queued moves precede the seam and must play. A mute is not a relocation, but it owes the same chase: a muted track (one with no pre-fader send to a bus) and a muted clip with a stored lane post nothing, not even a release, so the scheduler records a muted track by track (`noteStoredControllersTrackMuteWithheld`) and every muted MIDI clip whose non-empty lane overlaps the half-open window by clip (`noteStoredControllersClipMuteWithheld`), and runs the relocation's restore at the start of the window that ends a record, once: a track mute ends in the first window the track schedules again, whatever muted clips overlap it (the restore leaves them out), and a clip's mute in the first window that clip is no longer muted, no longer overlaps (ended or moved) or no longer exists, whatever other clips stay muted, which a track-mute restore before it does not discharge; the record lives as long as the engagement record, cleared by `releaseStoredControllers` and on dispose, so a stop or seek owes nothing to the next start. A live note that has no samples (a zero-length note) is not posted to a worklet instrument, as the export skips it: its release would sit on its own start frame and cut a different note of that pitch struck there.
- A bar number becomes a beat only through `getBarStartBeat`, the exact inverse of `getBarBeatAtPosition` at bar starts. Bars are counted the way that function counts them, so a bar a mid-bar meter change shortens shares its number with the bar the change opens and opens on its shortened piece. A flat `numerator * 4 / denominator` per bar is wrong as soon as the meter changes.
