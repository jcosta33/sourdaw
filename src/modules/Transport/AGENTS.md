# Transport module — Agent Guidelines

Playback lifecycle and control (play, stop, seek, record, overdub), playhead positioning & high-frequency scheduler, tempo maps and ramps, time signature maps, metronome, loop/punch regions, master gain headroom, and PPQ/sample position projections; does not own the WebAudio graph (AudioEngine) or arrangement track/clip items (Arrangement).

## Public Contract Surface

- `useCases`:
    - **Playback & Transport Controls**: `setPlayback`, `stopPlayback`, `isTransportIdle`, `countSettledTakes`, `togglePlayback`, `seekPlayhead`, `panicAllNotes`, `toggleRecording`, `toggleOverdub`, `setLoopRegion`, `toggleLoop`, `disableLooping`, `toggleMetronome`, `setMetronomeVolume`, `setCountInBars`, `toggleCountIn`, `setPreRollBars`, `togglePreRoll`, `setPunchIn`, `setPunchOut`, `togglePunchEnabled`, `createPunchRegionPatch`.
    - **Tempo & Time Signature Mapping**: `setTempo`, `setTimeSignature`, `addTempoChange`, `removeTempoChange`, `updateTempoChange`, `replaceTempoMap`, `resolveTempoAtBeat`, `shiftTimelineMapsAfterBeat`, `prepareTimelineMapStateRestore`, `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation`, `detectProjectTempo`, `adjustTempoPoint`, `addTimeSignatureChange`, `removeTimeSignatureChange`, `replaceTimeSignatureMap`, `getTimeSignatureAtBeat`, `getBarStartBeat`.
    - **Projections & Master Level**: `createMusicalPositionProjector`, `createSamplePositionProjector`, `projectPpqEndpoints`, `secondsBetweenBeats`, `setMasterGain`, `replaceMasterGain`, `ensureTrackStrips`, `getSchedulerTimingDiagnostics`, `reconcileVcaGroupRuntimeGain`, `reconcileVcaRuntimeGain`, `resolvePlaybackNoteReceiver`, `setStopPlaybackCallback`, `restoreTransportSnapshot`, `restoreTimelineMapSnapshot`, `getTransportHandlers`, `getTransportState`, `getTempoMapState`, `resolveTempoFieldState`, `updateTransportState`, `defaultTransportState`.
- `stores`: `transportStore` (`TransportState`, `MIN_TEMPO`, `MAX_TEMPO`), `tempoMapStore` (`TempoMapStoreState`), `timeSignatureMapStore` (`TimeSignatureMapStoreState`), `playheadPositionRef`, and the map readers `readTempoAtBeat`, `readSecondsAtBeat`, `readBeatAtSamples`, `readBarStartBeat` for handlers that cannot reach the use-case barrel.
- Handlers: `getTransportHandlers`.

## Key Subsystems

- **Playhead Scheduler**: Precise audio-clock scheduling loop backed by `playheadPositionRef` for zero-allocation, high-frequency position polling by UI canvas renderers.
- **Tempo Map Engine**: Resolves dynamic tempo changes, linear ramps, and metric beat conversions across project timelines (`useCases/tempoMap/*`).
- **Master Gain & VCA Reconciliation**: Controls master fader gain with headroom constraints, undo integration, and VCA group gain summing.

## Invariants & Traps

- Two tempo changes on one beat mean "arrive at the first, govern from the last", and list order is what says so. `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation` writes the pair when Delete Time cuts a linear ramp: the tempo before the cut never changes, so the ramp keeps its slope up to the cut and arrives at an instant change holding the value it reached, and the tempo steps there to the one carried from the span end. Every reader of the list therefore reads a segment that ends on a shared beat at the first change (the ramp's target) and one that starts there at the last (the governing change): `getTempoAtBeat`, `secondsBetweenBeats`, the Yeast copy of that integrator in `MidiEvent.ts`, `addTempoChange` (which rewrites the last), and `projectEngineTransportMaps` (which opens one engine segment per beat, because the engine refuses segments that do not start on strictly increasing frames). A project saved with a step reads wrongly in a build that predates this rule.
- Delete Time classifies each tempo and meter change against the user's exact bounds `S` and `E`, and `BEAT_EPSILON` applies only to that classification: a change before `S − ε` is kept, one from `S − ε` up to `E − ε` is removed, and one from `E − ε` on shifts by exactly `E − S`, the span the arrangement's clips, markers and sections move by, landing exactly on `S` when it lands within ε of it. The bounds never move to meet a nearby change: a span snapped to a change would shift the maps by a different amount from the content and put every later change and downbeat off its material. After classification every change on the cut sits exactly on `S` and every kept change more than ε before it, so each later "is there a change on the cut" decision compares exactly, and a ramp whose target is removed with the cut is read along its own line up to `S`, never past its target: a target within ε before `S` is where the ramp has already arrived, so it reaches `S` at that target's tempo (its slope before the cut changes by at most `(S − T) / (S − G)` of its rise) rather than overshooting by an extrapolation that a ramp narrower than ε turns into hundreds of BPM.
- Insert Time at `A` for `d` beats never changes the tempo or meter before `A`, moves the material from `A` on by exactly `d` with its tempo, and fills `[A, A + d)` with the tempo arriving at `A` (the value a ramp under way there has reached). Unlike Delete Time it classifies each tempo and meter change exactly, as the arrangement classifies clips and markers: one before `A` is kept and one from `A` on shifts by exactly `d`, with no tolerance. A tolerant classification would move a change a whole inserted span away from the clip or marker sitting on its own beat (a change at `A − 5e-7` would land on `A + d` while its clip stays). When the change kept last before `A` is linear and ramps toward a change at or past `A`, shifting that target alone would re-aim the ramp and slow everything before `A`, so `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation` writes an instant arrival at `A` holding the value the ramp reached there (placed before any change on that beat, so the ramp arrives at it). A target the shift puts past `A + d` gets a linear change at `A + d` holding the same value, which resumes the ramp toward the shifted target on its original slope; a target the shift puts on `A + d` (one on `A`, or a rounding step past it) arrives there itself and governs from there, since a continuation on its beat would govern after it. A steady change in force at `A` (instant, flat, or the last change) needs neither, and the insert only shifts. Insert Time keeps bar-anchored material on its bars, as Delete Time does: every downbeat of the original meter grid at or after `A` lands exactly `d` beats later, every one before `A` is unchanged, and the meter in force on each beat of the moved material keeps its value. Bars are counted from the change that opens them, so shifting the changes alone would leave the grid after `A + d` on its old phase whenever `d` is not a whole number of the bars in force at `A`, and moved material would sit mid-bar (wrong bar number, metronome accent and bar snapping). `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation` therefore writes, after any change already on that beat, a meter change at `B + d` carrying the meter in force at `A`, where `B` is the start of the bar `A` falls in (`A` itself when it is a downbeat): the bar from `B` runs on, as a partial bar, up to `B + d`, so every moved position, not only the downbeats, keeps its beat in the bar. The carry starts at the bar start because the material between `B` and the next downbeat moves too, and a grid restarted at the next downbeat's shifted position would put it a bar late. `B + d` can only be written when it is not before `A`; a span shorter than the gap back to `B` would put the change in material that does not move, so the grid resumes at the first original downbeat at or after `A`, shifted by `d`, and only the downbeats of the moved material are kept. It writes none when the shifted grid already has a downbeat there (`d` is a whole number of those bars) or when a meter change that shifts onto it opens its own bar; a later meter change keeps shifting with its material, and the carry is still written before it when it falls ahead of the next downbeat. Duplicate Time Range inserts its copy through the same operation. Undo restores the exact previous meter map and redo re-creates the carried change.
- High-frequency playhead updates during live playback MUST read from `playheadPositionRef` — NEVER push per-frame playhead positions into `transportStore` or React component state.
- Tempo values must stay within the range their own validator enforces: the transport's base tempo within `MIN_TEMPO` (20 BPM) and `MAX_TEMPO` (300 BPM) owned by `stores/transportStore`, and a tempo-map change within `MIN_TEMPO_MAP_TEMPO` (20 BPM) and `MAX_TEMPO_MAP_TEMPO` (999 BPM) owned by `models/TempoMap`. The two ranges deliberately differ.
- `stopPlayback` is one transport publication: it clears `isRecording` (through `stopActiveRecording`) and `isPlaying` inside one store batch, so a subscriber never sees a rolling, not-recording transport that the user never produced — the state in which Auto input monitoring would close and reopen its edge. A punch-out (`stopActiveRecording` alone) is a real publication of that state.
- A stop that closes a take is observable after the transport state says idle. A user's Pause or Stop writes `isPlaying: false` and `isRecording: false` synchronously and then awaits the recorder's flush and the take's commit, so `recordingLifecycle` registers every `stopActiveRecording` as closing for as long as it runs (`trackClosing`) and counts every take commit when it settles (`settledCommitCount`, fed by `trackCommit`, which also marks a commit in flight). `isTransportIdle` is false while any stop is closing or any commit is in flight, and a later stop waits for every earlier closing stop (`waitForClosing`) because `waitForCommits` clears its set when it snapshots, so a second stop's own flush finds the recorder already stopped and could resolve before the first stop's terminal registered its commit. Only a stop may call `waitForCommits`, since the commits it snapshots are no longer there for anyone else to wait on (#4439). `countSettledTakes` exposes that count and nothing else: Transport does not know which takes a project has saved, so a project replacement compares the count with the one Project recorded at its last save, discard or switch, and never waits on the lifecycle itself.
- All timeline edits that insert or delete time ranges must invoke `prepareTimelineMapTimeOperation` (insert holds the tempo arriving at the insert point across the inserted span and carries the meter in force there from the start of the bar it falls in, shifted by the span; delete carries the tempo and meter in force at the span end to its start) to keep tempo and time signature markers synchronized with track content. `shiftTimelineMapsAfterBeat` only shifts the changes past a beat and keeps no ramp, so it is not an Insert Time.
- Stored clip controllers (Grand Boule pedals, Levain controllers) are placed by `projectClipControllerEvents` — the one projection live scheduling and the offline render share — over the same half-open `[fromBeat, toBeat)` window as notes, at the sample frame a note on that beat gets. The one exception is a pedal lift on a clip's closing line — sustain (CC64) only at value 0, since it is a continuous position and a partial value there is no lift, and the other switch controllers (CC65 to CC69) below the latch threshold of `pianoPedalController`: it plays on the clip end, as the last thing the clip does (a sustain lift drawn on the final bar line ends the sustain before the next clip), while every other row there (a press, a partial sustain, a continuous controller) stays dropped, because a press on the last line would hold past the clip end with nothing left to hold (a comp cut or trim landing on a press would leave the pedal down until stop); the window that closes at or past the clip end owns it, because a window opening on the clip end does not schedule the clip; an inner loop seam stays half-open, and notes keep the half-open end everywhere. At one frame the instrument receives releases, then the controllers of a clip closing on that frame (its closing-line release and its own earlier moves there, in its own order, as the export keeps them), then other controllers, then note-ons, then note expression (`SAME_FRAME_EVENT_ORDER`), so the head of a clip starting where another ends has the last word whatever the clip order, in live playback (`sameFramePostQueue` posts a track's window in that order, so it cannot depend on which window a note started in or which clip came first) and in the offline render (`comparePendingWorkletEvents`) alike, and no note is posted whose release would share its own start frame. The reason: a pedal must catch a note struck at its frame, and sostenuto, una corda and Levain dynamics must apply to the note struck there, but a pedal must not catch a note released at its frame; the engine queues keep posting order within a frame (live MIDI input stamps several performer events on one frame), so the order is made before the post, never inside the queue. Playback that stops or seeks ends its hold on the instruments through `releaseStoredControllers` (reached through `stopPlayheadScheduler`): it first drops the stored moves still queued for a frame that will not come, then lifts every pedal stored playback moved, frameless, and never writes a controller that is not a pedal (CC1, CC7 and CC11 keep the value they last applied). The drop is needed because `allNotesOff` keeps queued Grand Boule pedals and every queued Levain controller (a controller is state, not a note), so a lift or a CC11 move posted for the up-to-0.1 s look-ahead past the old position would otherwise apply after a jump's restore or after the stop. Every move stored playback posts carries a `stored` mark through `grandBouleControls`/`levainControls`, and `discardStoredPedals`/`discardStoredCc` drop only marked moves (queue compaction in place, nothing allocated on the audio thread; a stored frameless move also leaves a performer's queued moves alone), so live MIDI input, whose order within a frame must survive, is never discarded or reordered. Only devices the scheduler itself posted to are in the record, so a pedal the user holds live is never touched. The lift covers every pedal moved, not only those whose last posted move was engaged, because a dropped queued lift leaves the pedal where the last applied move put it, which the post-time record cannot tell. Sending the value in force when playback starts mid-clip (or after a user seek) is the transport's chase, not this projection's. A playback-internal relocation is not a start: a loop wrap (scheduled seam and late wrap) and a follow-action jump open their window with `scheduleMidiNotes(..., opensAtRelocation)`, which sends, at the destination frame, the value in force there for every stored controller of the track (`projectClipControllerRestore`, a track-level chase as a DAW does it: per controller the last move up to the destination across every pass of every clip of the track that plays, a clip that does not span the destination included and projected only up to the destination, which is the value continuous playback holds there, so a pass head that carries nothing (no row of the controller precedes its visible span) leaves the value the previous pass ended on and is never reset, and a clip that follows one without a row for a controller leaves it set; the clips are those the window scheduler would play (`resolveStoredControllerClips`: comping and mute rules included, in the window's clip order; a MIDI clip's stored controllers play whether or not it has a note list, the window, the relocation restore and the export alike treating a clip with no list as one with no notes). "Last" is the order the window posts in: by sample frame, then the moves of a clip closing on that frame ahead of other clips' moves there, then clip sequence, then the clip's own row order, never by float beat, because two rows a rounding step apart in beat share a frame and the instrument ends on the later clip's. A controller is one value per instrument, the post path drops the channel, so it is keyed by number alone: the last row on any channel carries, and a controller whose last move is one the window posts on the destination's sample frame is left to the window, so it is sent once. Every event comes from `projectClipControllerEvents`, the projection the window emits through, and "at the destination" is a sample-frame comparison: the restore never computes a pass, a content beat or a tolerance itself, because any second computation of a beat disagrees with the window by a rounding step when the clip start or content offset is not a dyadic fraction) and returns to rest only what stored playback moved that no row has a value for there: a pedal is lifted, and a Levain CC1, CC2, CC7 or CC11 goes back to the engine's own default (`LEVAIN_CONTROLLER_DEFAULTS`, a mirror of `ExpressionState::new` pinned to the Rust source), because a controller that stays where a pass left it is as stuck as a pedal. Stored playback records the non-pedal controllers it moves as it records pedals. A destination where no clip of the track plays is no different (a gap is not a reset, so the pedal a clip before it left down stays down): the restore runs for the device the window routes stored controllers to, whether or not stored playback has posted to it yet, and the window and the restore share that one decision (`resolveStoredControllerDevice`: the honouring worklet synth, and neither a Yeast, drum-kit nor Toaster route). `releaseUnrestoredStoredControllers` lifts, unconditionally, only what no track restored: a muted or frozen track, or a posted device the scheduler no longer routes stored controllers to. The reason: a relocation stops notes but deliberately keeps pedal state, so without it a sustain pressed late in one pass stays down through every later pass. One rule serves every relocation: a pedal with a row in force is given that value and never lifted first (a Grand Boule releases the voices a lifted sustain was holding, and a press on the same frame does not bring them back). A loop-region or tempo-map edit during playback cuts the look-ahead and re-emits it from where playback stands (or from the loop start), so every arm of that teardown marks the re-emitting window a relocation (`reemitAfterEdit`). The late wrap (both arms), the jump and the edit run `discardStaleStoredMoves` before that window, which drops the stored moves queued for the old position and nothing else, so none applies over the restore; the scheduled seam never does, because its dying window's own queued moves precede the seam and must play. A mute is not a relocation, but it owes the same chase: a muted track (one with no pre-fader send to a bus) and a muted clip with a stored lane (controllers or pitch bend) post nothing, not even a release, so the scheduler records a muted track by track (`noteStoredControllersTrackMuteWithheld`) and every muted MIDI clip whose non-empty lane overlaps the half-open window by clip (`noteStoredControllersClipMuteWithheld`), and runs the relocation's restore at the start of the window that ends a record, once: a track mute ends in the first window the track schedules again, whatever muted clips overlap it (the restore leaves them out), and a clip's mute in the first window that clip is no longer muted, no longer overlaps (ended or moved) or no longer exists, whatever other clips stay muted, which a track-mute restore before it does not discharge; the record lives as long as the engagement record, cleared by `releaseStoredControllers` and on dispose, so a stop or seek owes nothing to the next start. A live note that has no samples (a zero-length note) is not posted to a worklet instrument, as the export skips it: its release would sit on its own start frame and cut a different note of that pitch struck there.
- A note's recorded expression changes (`projectNoteExpressionCurves`) are posted by the scheduler window whose half-open beat range contains them: the window that starts a worklet note posts its note-on and its release and keeps the note in `heldNoteExpression` with its own curve points, and the track's windows (the start window too, once its notes have started) post what falls in their range, on the frames the start window placed them (the clock its release was placed on, frozen at the time the note started, so a later window cannot slide a change past the release). A window that opens at a relocation (`opensAtRelocation`: loop seam, late wrap, jump, edit re-emit), a stop, a seek and a dispose forget the changes the notes' own curves parked, so a note a seek lands inside (never started) posts no change, and a note crossing the loop end posts none past the seam, where they would land on a same-pitch note starting the next pass. The late wrap, the jump, the edit re-emit, a stop, a seek and a dispose also end the notes themselves (`stopAllScheduled`), so the scheduler empties the registry beside it (`forgetHeldNoteExpression`); only the scheduled seam leaves notes sounding, and the channel bend rule below carries those on. A parked change inside a window that ran is posted by it and never later. A change that fell in windows the track did not run (a mute, or every clip of it muted) is never posted at its own, passed, frame; but the instrument was told nothing of it, so the window the track resumes in gives each note still sounding at its start the last such change, the full expression in force there, at the frame that window opens on and ahead of the window's own changes (a note released before the window is over and gets nothing). A bypass or a swap of the track's receiving instrument panics its notes outside the transport teardown, so every window of a MIDI track, muted or not, first forgets the parked changes of notes the current non-bypassed receiver did not voice.
- Stored channel pitch bend (the `MidiPitchBend` rows an ordinary pitch wheel records) plays wherever stored controllers do: a live window, a relocation, the window a mute ends in, and the offline render. It has one projection, `projectChannelBend`, built on `projectClipPitchBendEvents`, which places a row by the passes, content offset and carry rule `projectClipControllerEvents` uses, over the clips `resolveChannelBendClips` admits (the clips `resolveStoredControllerClips` admits through `resolvePlayedMidiClips`: a muted, comped-away or non-MIDI clip is left out, a clip with no note list is in, and bends the notes of the clips around it). It is delivered the way the live input delivers a bend on a non-member channel, as the sounding note's expression through `applyNoteExpression`, and not as a controller: Grand Boule, Fermenter and Levain bend a held voice by note and channel, hold no channel bend of their own, and reset a voice's bend on its note-on. So a note struck while a bend is in force, and holding none of its own, starts with the track's last move at or before its note-on frame (from any earlier clip of the track, clip end included), and a note sounding when a move arrives is given it at its frame. The start window reads the bend in force at a note's start from the clips whole (`startNoteExpression`). The moves after the start are not parked with the note, because the clips can change while it sounds (a mute, a comp, an edit of the lane): every window of the track reads the moves that fall in its own `[fromBeat, toBeat)` from the clips it admits then (`resolveChannelBendClips`, with the current mute, comping and rows) and gives them to every note of the track still sounding at each move's frame, on that note's pitch, channel and clock (`advanceNoteExpression` through `heldNoteExpression`), so a lane muted or edited after a note started moves it no further than the clips now say, and one unmuted mid-note moves it. What a note parks is only its own curve points. The window that resumes a track or a clip after a mute (a muted clip's pitch-bend lane is recorded by the mute record as its controllers are) reads the moves from the start of the timeline and gives each sounding note the bend in force at the frame the window opens on, ahead of the window's own changes, as the stored controllers' chase does. That listing hands over moves a sounding note already followed, so a held timeline records the beat of the latest event it has taken (`advancedBeat`) and skips a channel move at or before it: a move the note took, or one its own bend point has since overtaken, is never replayed over that point, while the chase still posts the expression the timeline holds at the frame the window opens on. A move on the release frame is dropped with the voice, a move on the start frame is the note-on's own expression, and moves that share a frame collapse to the last. A bend struck on a note-on's frame is applied by the `expression` slot of `SAME_FRAME_EVENT_ORDER`, behind the note-on, because a per-voice update addresses only a voice that exists and the controllers ahead of the note-on reach no voice; the note still sounds bent from its first sample, which is what that order protects for a pedal. The latest event holds the bend and its range, as the live input does (`handleWebMidiPitchBend`: last write wins). At a note's start its own recorded bend (its `pitchBend` scalar, or a curve point at or before the start) wins when it has one, whatever bend is in force, and the channel bend applies otherwise. While it sounds, a channel move holds the bend at the move's depth until the note's next own bend point, and that point holds it at the note's depth until the next channel move; a pressure or slide point is not a bend point and leaves the bend where it stands, and a move and a bend point on one frame leave the note's. So the stale centred bend before an MPE note leaves its glide alone. The note's pressure and slide stay its own. Bend is a continuous position, like CC1: nothing resets it at a clip end, a row exactly on the closing line is dropped, and the clip after one that ended bent starts bent. "Last" is by sample frame, then clip sequence, then row order, never float beat, as the controller restore orders. The depth is the RPN 0 that the same clip's controller rows declared before the move, run through the live input's own state machine (`createStoredBendRangeReader`), else ±2 (MIDI 1.0; the ±48 of `getDefaultBendRangeSemitones` is the MPE member default and never reads a channel bend); a range declared before a take began, or in another clip, is not in the rows. There is no engine-side bend to return to centre, so a stop, a seek and a dispose forget what a playback parked (`forgetHeldNoteExpression`) and a relocation forgets the notes' own parked points, and the notes struck afterwards start with the bend in force at their own start frame, computed from the rows: a jump into a bend-out span starts the note struck there bent, and one past the return starts it at centre. A note still sounding when the window opens at a relocation that silenced nothing (the scheduled loop seam: its dying window ends at the loop end, the incoming one opens at the loop start, and `stopAllScheduled` runs for neither) keeps its voice, and with it the bend it took before the loop end, which no later move of the dying pass would reach: the window gives each such note that follows the channel bend the bend in force at the destination, as a note struck there gets it, posted on the frame the window opens on and ahead of the notes it starts (`relocateHeldNoteExpression`, with `relocateNoteExpression`). No bend in force there is a bend at centre, at the depth the note was bent at, and a note already holding the bend in force is sent nothing. The moves of the window then reach it as they reach any sounding note, placed on the relocated window's clock (frozen when it opened) against the frame the note's release was posted on, which does not move: the move that lands after the release is dropped with the voice. The note's own curve points are not carried, since they belong to the pass it left. The registry therefore holds a note across the seam only while its release frame is after the frame the window opens on and the track's instrument is still the one that voiced it; the release is then placed in the new pass's beats, for the windows that follow to drop it as they drop any ended note. The carry runs for every track before any track's window starts a note (`carrySoundingNotes`), so what the track's own window does or skips at the seam does not decide it: a track in a gap at the loop start, with every clip there muted, or muted outright still has its notes ringing in the instrument, so each gets the bend in force at the destination (centre when no unmuted clip holds one) on the frame the window opens on. A note of a muted track stays registered, and the window that ends the mute (or the clip's mute) gives it the bend in force then, as it does any sounding note. A note is forgotten, not carried, only where nothing of the instrument sounds it: a frozen track plays its buffer, a bypass or swap leaves the instrument that voiced it, and a track that no longer exists or is not a MIDI track has none; a Yeast track and an instrument that sounds no pitch bend never registered one. A bend reaches only an instrument that sounds pitch bend (`getNoteExpressionDimensions`), never on a Yeast track (as for stored controllers), and never through the drum kit, Toaster, built-in synth or Faust path, which do not use the worklet note path; the render applies the same gate through the strategy's `noteExpression`, and takes the projection by injection (`projectChannelBend`, set at the composition root) as it takes `projectClipControllers`. The render orders the moves by the frame the timeline gives them, which stays below zero before an export region's start instead of clamping there as a note's does, so the bend in force at the region start is the timeline's last, not the last clip of the track's clip list.
- A bar number becomes a beat only through `getBarStartBeat`, the exact inverse of `getBarBeatAtPosition` at bar starts. Bars are counted the way that function counts them, so a bar a mid-bar meter change shortens shares its number with the bar the change opens and opens on its shortened piece. A flat `numerator * 4 / denominator` per bar is wrong as soon as the meter changes.
